Safety

The plan is built around three principles: a designated medical lead at all times, a buddy system that's never optional, and a confident protocol for the highest-risk activity (the survival overnight). Here's how we operate.

For parents: if you have a specific concern about your son's medical condition, medications, allergies, fears, or anything else — please contact a leader directly before the trip. Don't wait for the trailhead. We will accommodate.

Medical

Medical Lead

One leader carries the full first aid kit, a tourniquet, and bear spray at all times. They have professional medical experience and are the designated triage on any injury.

Medications

Bring all personal medications labeled with your name. Tell the medical lead at the trailhead what you brought and where it's stored. Anything time-sensitive (insulin, EpiPen, inhaler) stays on the boy who uses it, but the medical lead knows where it is.

Altitude

Camp is at 9,740 ft. Trailhead is at 10,260 ft. Drink more water than feels natural on day one. Mild headaches are common. Tell a leader if you feel lightheaded, nauseous, or short of breath.

Emergency Extraction

Leaders carry a SPOT GPS for emergency activation. Nearest hospital is in Heber City or Park City — roughly 90 min driving from the trailhead in good conditions. Confirm nearest with leader before departure

Wilderness Survival Night (Tuesday)

What it is: Tuesday evening, boys are placed in small groups (2–3) about 100 feet from main camp — within sight of leaders. Each group builds its own shelter, lights a small fire if permitted, and sleeps out. The goal is the experience of self-reliance, not real isolation.

What each group has

  • One backpacking stove + fuel
  • One water filter
  • Freeze-dried meals (1 dinner + 1 breakfast per boy)
  • One SPOT GPS device (emergency activation)
  • Their own sleeping bag + a tarp / paracord / hatchet

Leader Protocol

  • Leaders do a check-in at full dark (≈22:00).
  • Second check-in at midnight.
  • Any boy may walk back to main camp at any point without consequence.
  • Buddy system enforced — no boy is ever alone in their group.

What it isn't

Not Naked & Afraid. Boys are 100 ft from a warm tent, a hot meal, and four adults. The experience is intentional discomfort within a tight safety net.

Bear & Wildlife Protocol

The Uintas are black bear habitat. Cougars present but rare. The protocol is non-negotiable:

  • No food in tents. Ever. No food, no candy, no gum, no toothpaste in a tent.
  • All food locked away from sleeping area overnight. Preferred: Tim's enclosed trailer (if it makes the road). Fallback: sealed containers stored 50+ yards from camp, or hung in a tree (bear bag, 12+ ft up).
  • Cooking area separated from sleeping area by ~50 ft.
  • Bear spray with the medical leader at all times.
  • Make noise on the trail — talking, singing, occasional whistle blast. Surprised bears are dangerous bears.
  • If you see a bear: back away slowly, don't run. Group up. Speak in a calm, loud voice. Tell a leader immediately.

Firearms

At least one adult leader carries a sidearm for protection (large wildlife defense). Legal in National Forest; church policy is being verified for this specific trip. Final confirmation pending

Recreational shooting (BB guns, clay pigeons, archery) is an activity option for the boys, supervised by leaders. Confirmation that recreational shooting is permitted in this part of the Uinta-Wasatch-Cache National Forest is still being verified — fallback is BB guns + archery only, no firearms for the activity.

Buddy System

Always in pairs — minimum. Off to fish? Buddy. Going to the bathroom? Buddy. Walking around the lake? Buddy. The lake area is contained but kids wander faster than adults notice. Buddy is the rule.

Communications

From camp

  • No cell signal anywhere. Don't try.
  • Starlink at camp for non-emergency family contact (leaders have one).
  • SPOT GPS for emergency activation only.

For parents during the trip

If you need to reach your son in a true emergency, contact a leader on their cell — they'll get the message via Starlink during evening check-in windows. Specific contact protocol to be confirmed in the parent meeting

Water Safety

  • Personal flotation device required for paddle boards and the inflatable boat. Confirm PFD count
  • Always swim / paddle in groups, with a leader present.
  • The lake is cold — short swim sessions, dry off, warm up.
  • No swimming after dark.

Fire

  • Leaders verify current Forest Service fire restrictions on arrival.
  • Solo Stove (contained burn) preferred where allowed.
  • No open ground fires unless permitted.
  • All fires fully out — drown, stir, drown again — before sleep.

Wildfire History — Our Area

Wildfire is a real factor in the Uintas every summer. Three fires from the last decade are worth knowing about, and only one even touched our basin — but it didn't reach our specific route or campsite.

2018 — Murdock Fire (5,600 acres)

Lightning-ignited in late August 2018, in our basin. The fire moved north from its ignition point up the Duchesne River gorge, crossing the Highline Trail and reaching the west flank of Mt. Agassiz. Mirror Lake Highway closed for several days. Our route (Fehr Lake → Sheppard → Hoover → Echo Lake) was not in the burn area. Google Earth Photo Spheres from July 2024 and AllTrails photos from September 2025 confirm the trail and lake areas are intact, with healthy forest along the entire corridor.

2020 — East Fork Fire (89,000 acres)

The largest Utah fire of 2020. Started above Upper Stillwater Reservoir, burned past Moon Lake. Same south slope as our trip but ~15–20 miles east, in a different drainage. No impact on our area.

2025 — Beulah Fire (5,700 acres)

Last summer (August 2025). On the north slope of the Uintas near the Wyoming border — completely different watershed, separated from us by the Uinta Crest. Closed Hinckley Scout Ranch. Did not affect our area, but it's a reminder that summer fires happen with little warning.

Fire Contingency Plan

  • Fire restrictions are likely tight in July. Plan as if open fires won't be allowed. Solo Stove + propane Blackstones are the fallback.
  • Smoke from regional fires can degrade air quality even when our area has no active fire. Asthma inhalers are encouraged.
  • Standing-dead trees from beetle-kill exist throughout the Uintas — not just in fire scars. Recon will scout the campsite for "widow-makers" overhead. Don't sleep under a dead tree.
  • Plan B campsite outside the Uintas is being identified in case an active fire closes UT-150 the week of the trip. Mirror Lake Highway has closed three times in the last seven summers (2018, 2020, 2025) due to fire.
  • Two weeks before the trip: leaders confirm current fire restrictions via utahfireinfo.gov and the Uinta-Wasatch-Cache fire alerts page.

Lightning & Storms

Afternoon thunderstorms are normal in the Uintas. If a storm rolls in:

  • Get off the lake immediately.
  • Off open ridges or exposed slopes.
  • Into trees or low ground — not under a single tall tree.
  • Wait it out. They usually pass in 30–60 min.
